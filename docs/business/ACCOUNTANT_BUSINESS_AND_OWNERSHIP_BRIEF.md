# Glacier — Accountant Business and Ownership Brief

## Status

Draft prepared 28 August 2026 for discussion with the accountant. It records current intentions and questions; it does not prescribe a legal, accounting, tax or trust structure.

## Purpose of the meeting

Obtain advice on establishing Glacier as a new Australian enterprise, separate from Ice Rinks Australia, and on safely holding or acquiring early assets while the final entity structure is established.

The immediate practical question is whether the existing trustee/trust can temporarily acquire Glacier domains and other foundational accounts, then transfer them to the intended Glacier entity without creating avoidable ownership, tax, duty, GST, related-party or recordkeeping problems.

## Business summary

Glacier is a software platform for event commerce and operations. Its first use case is session-based seasonal attractions, informed by the founder's Ice Rinks Australia operating experience, but it is intended to serve other event organisers and event categories.

The platform currently includes organiser event setup and reporting, public event websites and online booking, ticketing and scanning, products and inventory, payments, waivers, customer-service adjustments, walk-up POS sales, role-based staff access and flexible-ticket operations.

No real-world Glacier event is expected before approximately May 2027. The software remains in development and production infrastructure has not yet been provisioned.

## Intended commercial direction

Potential revenue models remain under evaluation and may include:

- per-ticket transaction fees;
- subscription or annual software fees;
- per-event or enterprise licences;
- payment-related margin where lawful and commercially appropriate;
- premium modules;
- setup, onboarding, support and training; and
- white-label licensing.

Glacier should be capable of contracting with Ice Rinks Australia as a pilot customer or related party without Ice Rinks Australia automatically becoming the owner of Glacier's domains, software, intellectual property or production accounts.

## Founder intentions requiring advice

- Treat Glacier as a new enterprise, not merely a product inside Ice Rinks Australia.
- Preserve the ability for Ice Rinks Australia to be a pilot customer and provide industry knowledge on documented terms.
- Keep future investment, licensing, sale or expansion options open.
- Ensure domains, source code, trademarks, cloud accounts, payment accounts and contracts sit with the appropriate long-term holder.
- Avoid unnecessary complexity where a simpler initial structure safely achieves these intentions.
- Establish clear control, succession and asset-transfer records from the beginning.

## Questions for the accountant

### Recommended structure

1. What initial structure best fits the intended risk, revenue, ownership and future-investment profile: a proprietary operating company, trust-owned company, trust operation, separate IP owner or another arrangement?
2. Who should own the shares in any new company and what are the tax, control, succession and asset-protection consequences?
3. Should the operating entity also own the software/IP initially, or is separation justified at this stage?
4. What registrations are required initially: company, ABN, GST, business name, PAYG or others?
5. When should GST registration occur given development costs, pre-revenue activity and possible ticket-based fees?

### Existing development and IP

6. Who currently owns the software and documentation created to date based on who performed, funded or commissioned the work?
7. Are assignments, licences or related-party agreements needed from the founder, Ice Rinks Australia, the trust or any contributor?
8. How should pre-incorporation development costs and founder contributions be recorded?
9. If assets are transferred to a new company, what valuation, consideration, capital-gains, GST, duty or loan-account consequences may arise?

### Temporary acquisition through the trustee/trust

10. Can the existing trustee/trust appropriately acquire a `.com`, eligible `.com.au`, business name or other early Glacier asset temporarily?
11. In whose exact legal name and capacity should each purchase and invoice be recorded?
12. Should the trustee hold the asset beneficially, on an express interim arrangement, or under another documented mechanism?
13. What written evidence should be created at purchase to support the intended later transfer?
14. What tax, GST, duty, valuation or related-party consequences could arise on transfer to the new Glacier entity?
15. Is it cleaner to wait for entity establishment unless a commercially important name is at immediate risk?

### Relationship with Ice Rinks Australia

16. Should Ice Rinks Australia be documented as pilot customer, development contributor, licensor/licensee, related-party service provider or some combination?
17. How should shared costs, staff time, equipment, data or know-how be invoiced or recorded?
18. What arm's-length or related-party documentation is appropriate before pilot use?

### Banking, payments and production accounts

19. Which entity should be the merchant/customer-contracting entity for online and POS payments?
20. Should event ticket proceeds flow through Glacier, directly to event organisers, or through a later payment-platform arrangement, and what accounting/regulatory advice is needed before deciding?
21. When should banking, Stripe and cloud billing accounts be created, and can any be transferred cleanly if opened early?
22. What bookkeeping categories and evidence should be maintained now for software development, domains, legal advice, cloud costs and founder funding?
