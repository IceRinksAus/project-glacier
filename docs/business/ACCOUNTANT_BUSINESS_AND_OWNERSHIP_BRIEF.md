# Glacier — Accountant Business and Ownership Brief

## Status

Draft prepared 28 August 2026 for discussion with the accountant. It records current intentions and questions; it does not prescribe a legal, accounting, tax or trust structure.

## Purpose of the meeting

Obtain advice on the most appropriate path for developing Glacier as a distinct commercial product, including whether it should be established immediately as a new Australian enterprise or initially incubated as a documented division/product of Ice Rinks Australia before a later spin-out.

The immediate practical questions are:

- whether using Ice Rinks Australia for the development and first controlled pilot materially simplifies cost and administration;
- how Glacier assets, costs, revenue, liabilities and data should be separated in the records if that path is used;
- how a future spin-out should be documented from the beginning; and
- alternatively, whether the existing trustee/trust can temporarily acquire Glacier domains and other foundational accounts, then transfer them to the intended Glacier entity without avoidable ownership, tax, duty, GST, related-party or recordkeeping problems.

## Business summary

Glacier is a software platform for event commerce and operations. Its first use case is session-based seasonal attractions, informed by the founder's Ice Rinks Australia operating experience, but it is intended to serve other event organisers and event categories.

The platform currently includes organiser event setup and reporting, public event websites and online booking, ticketing and scanning, products and inventory, payments, waivers, customer-service adjustments, walk-up POS sales, role-based staff access and flexible-ticket operations.

No real-world Glacier event is expected before approximately May 2027. The software remains in development and production infrastructure has not yet been provisioned.

## Intended commercial direction

Potential revenue models remain under evaluation and may include:

- per-ticket transaction fees;
- subscription or annual software fees;
- per-event or enterprise licences;
- payment-related margin where lawful and commercially appropriate;
- premium modules;
- setup, onboarding, support and training; and
- white-label licensing.

If Glacier becomes independent, it should be capable of contracting with Ice Rinks Australia as a pilot customer or related party. If it is incubated inside Ice Rinks Australia, asset ownership and the basis of any later transfer must instead be recorded explicitly.

## Structural pathways under consideration

### Option A — Independent Glacier entity now

Establish the advised Glacier company/ownership structure before acquiring permanent domains, production accounts, customer contracts or payment relationships.

This provides the cleanest separation but creates establishment, accounting, legal and administration costs before the product has completed a real-world pilot.

### Option B — Glacier as an Ice Rinks Australia division/product

Ice Rinks Australia owns and operates Glacier, contracts with pilot customers, holds the assets and recognises all income, expenses and liabilities.

This may be the least expensive immediate approach, but could make a later separation more complex and may expose Glacier assets to the operating risks of Ice Rinks Australia. It also requires clarity about whether Glacier is intended to remain inside that business permanently.

### Option C — Documented incubation with intended future spin-out

Ice Rinks Australia operates the development and first controlled pilot while Glacier is tracked as a distinct cost centre/product. Domains, code, brand, cloud accounts, contracts and development contributions are entered in a dedicated asset register. A written arrangement records the intended treatment of those assets and the mechanism or decision gate for a later transfer to a standalone entity.

This is the founder's current preferred pathway for professional evaluation. It is not adopted until the accountant and commercial lawyer confirm that its tax, ownership, liability, insurance, privacy and transfer implications are acceptable.

## Questions for the accountant

### Recommended structure

1. Compare the cost, tax, risk, administration and future-transfer consequences of Options A, B and C above.
2. Is documented incubation within Ice Rinks Australia appropriate for the development and first controlled pilot, or does a material reason justify forming Glacier independently now?
3. What objective trigger should cause a spin-out: first external customer, first live payment, revenue threshold, investment, liability exposure or another event?
4. What initial standalone structure would best fit the intended risk, revenue, ownership and future-investment profile: a proprietary operating company, trust-owned company, trust operation, separate IP owner or another arrangement?
5. Who should own the shares in any new company and what are the tax, control, succession and asset-protection consequences?
6. Should the operating entity also own the software/IP initially, or is separation justified at this stage?
7. What registrations are required initially: company, ABN, GST, business name, PAYG or others?
8. When should GST registration occur given development costs, pre-revenue activity and possible ticket-based fees?

### Existing development and IP

9. Who currently owns the software and documentation created to date based on who performed, funded or commissioned the work?
10. Are assignments, licences or related-party agreements needed from the founder, Ice Rinks Australia, the trust or any contributor?
11. How should historical and future development costs and founder contributions be recorded?
12. If Ice Rinks Australia incubates Glacier, should code, domains and brand be owned by it, licensed to it, or governed under another documented arrangement?
13. If assets are later transferred to a new company, what valuation, consideration, capital-gains, GST, duty or loan-account consequences may arise?

### Temporary acquisition through the trustee/trust

14. If Ice Rinks Australia is not the appropriate interim holder, can the existing trustee/trust appropriately acquire a `.com`, eligible `.com.au`, business name or other early Glacier asset temporarily?
15. In whose exact legal name and capacity should each purchase and invoice be recorded?
16. Should the trustee hold the asset beneficially, on an express interim arrangement, or under another documented mechanism?
17. What written evidence should be created at purchase to support the intended later transfer?
18. What tax, GST, duty, valuation or related-party consequences could arise on transfer to the new Glacier entity?
19. Is it cleaner to wait for entity establishment unless a commercially important name is at immediate risk?

### Relationship with Ice Rinks Australia

20. Under each pathway, should Ice Rinks Australia be documented as owner/operator, pilot customer, development contributor, licensor/licensee, related-party service provider or some combination?
21. If Glacier is a division/product, what separate cost centre, asset register, management accounts and internal approvals should be maintained?
22. How should shared costs, staff time, equipment, data or know-how be invoiced or recorded?
23. What documentation should exist before pilot use to preserve a clean later spin-out?

### Banking, payments and production accounts

24. Which entity should be the merchant/customer-contracting entity for online and POS payments during incubation and after a spin-out?
25. Should event ticket proceeds flow through Glacier, directly to event organisers, or through a later payment-platform arrangement, and what accounting/regulatory advice is needed before deciding?
26. When should banking, Stripe and cloud billing accounts be created, and can any be transferred cleanly if opened early?
27. What bookkeeping categories and evidence should be maintained now for software development, domains, legal advice, cloud costs and founder funding?
