# Glacier Unit Economics and Viability — Initial Decision Model

## Status

Draft prepared 28 August 2026 after reviewing Ice Rinks Australia's approximate prior-season volume and updated 1 September 2026. This is a decision model, not an approved forecast or valuation.

The organiser has decided to continue product development under a no-material-new-spend gate until the Product Comfort Gate is complete. Small reversible items may be approved individually. A national Winter Festival is a potential upside scenario, not a guaranteed volume forecast. Ice Rinks Australia is the provisional incubation path if an initial testing season proceeds.

## Executive conclusion

At approximately 25,000 annual Tickets and an avoided provider fee of $1 per Ticket, Glacier creates at most $25,000 per year of directly observable gross economic value for Ice Rinks Australia before considering any other benefits.

That volume does **not** currently support Glacier as a convincing standalone single-client software company carrying the full independent-entity overhead. Under documented incubation inside Ice Rinks Australia, Glacier may save a modest amount or approximately break even on recurring cash costs, but the result becomes weak after allocating establishment cost, founder/development effort, support risk and future replacement work.

The position changes materially if Ice Rinks Australia proceeds with a national Winter Festival comparable to its prior national programme. User-supplied historical performance for that programme was approximately 130,000 Tickets, 112,000 extras such as Kangas and ride Tickets, and more than $3.6 million in total revenue. At that scale, Glacier becomes potentially viable even before external clients, subject to updated cost, support and execution assumptions.

Glacier therefore needs at least one of the following to become a commercially attractive ongoing concern:

- materially more Ticket volume through a national programme and/or additional organisers;
- a fee above $1 per Ticket;
- a base Event/Organisation subscription in addition to transaction fees;
- demonstrable operating savings or revenue uplift beyond replacing the provider fee;
- licensing/enterprise revenue; or
- a deliberately limited role as an internal Ice Rinks Australia operating asset rather than a standalone SaaS company.

## 1. Current comparison basis

User-supplied prior-season facts:

- approximately 25,000 Tickets sold;
- 123Tix retained approximately $1 per Ticket as its provider/platform fee; and
- Ice Rinks Australia bore the Stripe/payment-processing fees separately through deductions from Ticket income, leaving 123Tix's $1 fee as clear provider revenue.

Initial assumptions requiring invoice confirmation:

- the $1 applies to every completed Ticket, not every Booking;
- cancelled/refunded Tickets and GST treatment do not materially change the figure;
- no material provider subscription, setup or ancillary fees are omitted;
- Ice Rinks Australia bears substantially equivalent Stripe fees under either provider, now confirmed as the correct comparison basis; and
- Glacier does not initially charge Ice Rinks Australia an accounting profit margin separate from the avoided external fee.

Because Ice Rinks Australia paid Stripe fees separately under 123Tix and would continue to bear payment processing under Glacier, those fees are excluded from the provider-versus-Glacier incremental comparison. They remain important to Event economics and cash flow, but they are not a Glacier operating cost or saving unless Glacier changes the merchant/payment arrangement or secures different pricing.

## 2. Direct annual value at current volume

| Measure | Calculation | Annual amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| Avoided 123Tix provider fee | 25,000 Tickets × $1.00 | $25,000 |
| Stripe fees | Borne by Ice Rinks Australia under both alternatives | Excluded from Glacier profitability comparison |
| Maximum direct gross saving before Glacier costs | Provider fee avoided | $25,000 |

The $25,000 is not new external revenue in consolidated Ice Rinks Australia accounts if Glacier is an internal division. It is a genuine avoided external 123Tix expense and therefore improves Ice Rinks Australia's result dollar-for-dollar before Glacier's incremental costs. Internal cross-charges do not create group profit.

## 3. Incubation economics

Using the current budget baseline:

| Scenario | Annual incremental Glacier cost | Direct saving after recurring cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lean controlled operation | $12,000 | $13,000 positive |
| Mid planning case | $20,000 | $5,000 positive |
| Upper incubation allowance | $27,000 | $2,000 negative |

These figures exclude founder salary and the value of development work.

If $15,000–$35,000 of setup/pilot-readiness cost is allocated over three years, the annual allocation is approximately $5,000–$11,667. The resulting indicative position is:

| Scenario | Recurring cost | Three-year setup allocation | Indicative annual result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Lean | $12,000 | $5,000 | $8,000 positive |
| Mid | $20,000 | $8,333 | $3,333 negative |
| Upper | $27,000 | $11,667 | $13,667 negative |

The lean case may generate a small cash saving, but it does not yet provide a strong risk-adjusted return on the software already built or future operating responsibility.

## 4. Break-even Ticket volume

At a $1 fee/saving per Ticket:

| Cost basis | Break-even annual Tickets |
|---|---:|
| $12,000 recurring cost | 12,000 |
| $20,000 recurring cost | 20,000 |
| $27,000 recurring cost | 27,000 |
| $17,000 lean cost plus setup allocation | 17,000 |
| $28,333 mid cost plus setup allocation | 28,333 |
| $38,667 upper cost plus setup allocation | 38,667 |

At 25,000 Tickets, Glacier clears the lean threshold, approximately misses the mid fully allocated threshold and does not clear the upper threshold.

This demonstrates why infrastructure and assurance scope must be proportionate, but it does not justify removing controls required to protect real customers and Payments.

## 5. Standalone commercial scenarios

The following table illustrates gross platform fees before payment processing, support, sales, tax and customer-specific cost:

| Annual Tickets across all clients | $1/Ticket | $1.50/Ticket | $2/Ticket |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 25,000 | $25,000 | $37,500 | $50,000 |
| 50,000 | $50,000 | $75,000 | $100,000 |
| 100,000 | $100,000 | $150,000 | $200,000 |
| 250,000 | $250,000 | $375,000 | $500,000 |
| 500,000 | $500,000 | $750,000 | $1,000,000 |

A standalone Glacier company with approximately $25,000–$40,000 of fixed annual overhead could technically cross fixed-cost break-even around 25,000–40,000 Tickets at $1 each. That is not a sufficient commercial target because it leaves little or nothing for support labour, sales, product development, founder compensation, tax, customer acquisition, service incidents or return on investment.

As an initial planning hypothesis—not a validated target—standalone commercialisation should seek at least:

- 100,000 committed annual Tickets across multiple organisers; or
- an equivalent contracted revenue base combining subscription, setup/support and per-Ticket fees.

At $1 per Ticket, 100,000 Tickets creates $100,000 gross platform revenue. The remaining contribution must then be tested against support, growth, insurance, infrastructure and development cost.

## 6. National Winter Festival scenario

User-supplied historical reference case:

| Measure | Historical approximate volume |
|---|---:|
| Admission Tickets | 130,000 |
| Extras/add-ons | 112,000 |
| Combined units | 242,000 |
| Total Event revenue | More than $3.6 million |

### Direct provider-fee comparison

If the avoided provider fee remains $1 for each admission Ticket:

| Measure | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| Avoided admission provider fees | 130,000 × $1 | $130,000 |
| Independent Glacier annual fixed allowance | Current $25,000–$40,000 baseline | $(25,000)–$(40,000) |
| Indicative contribution before support growth, setup allocation and tax | Difference | **$90,000–$105,000** |

Cloud, support and assurance costs may rise above the present pilot allowance at national scale. Even allowing a higher $40,000–$60,000 annual operating envelope would leave an indicative $70,000–$90,000 before founder/staff compensation, setup recovery and tax.

This is a materially stronger base than the 25,000-Ticket case and could recover a controlled setup programme within one successful national season.

### Extras and add-ons

The 112,000 extras demonstrate significant platform value in Product Rules, Kanga capacity, merchandise inventory, POS and reporting. They do not automatically create Glacier fee revenue under the historical $1-per-admission comparison.

Possible future pricing sensitivities are shown only to inform commercial design:

| Extra/add-on platform fee | 112,000 extras | Additional gross fee |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $0.00 | 112,000 | $0 |
| $0.25 | 112,000 | $28,000 |
| $0.50 | 112,000 | $56,000 |
| $1.00 | 112,000 | $112,000 |

Charging per extra may not be the preferred customer proposition. Alternatives include an Event/season licence, base subscription, tiered volume pricing or a per-Booking fee. No add-on fee should enter the forecast until willingness to pay and competitive positioning are validated.

### Revenue sensitivity

The $3.6 million historical Event revenue is not Glacier revenue. A hypothetical platform fee based on gross Event sales would produce:

| Hypothetical platform rate | Gross platform fee on $3.6 million |
|---:|---:|
| 0.25% | $9,000 |
| 0.50% | $18,000 |
| 1.00% | $36,000 |

Percentage pricing could create complexity and may be less transparent than Ticket/subscription pricing. It is included only as sensitivity, not a recommendation.

### Decision implication

The national festival should become a formal commercialisation gate:

- if credible planning indicates approximately 100,000–130,000 annual admission Tickets, continued investment and a standalone Glacier structure become much easier to justify;
- if the programme remains uncertain and volume stays near 25,000, Glacier should remain a cost-controlled internal/incubated system;
- entity spin-out should follow credible committed Event volume, not optimistic projections alone; and
- a national deployment requires higher availability, support, device, recovery and incident capacity than the current small-pilot assumptions.

## 7. Value not yet quantified

Glacier may create value beyond the provider fee, but none should be included in the investment case without evidence:

- reduced manual reporting and reconciliation labour;
- faster POS/gate throughput and fewer staffing hours;
- increased add-on, merchandise or Flexible Ticket revenue;
- lower refund/error/fraud losses;
- better customer data and repeat marketing outcomes;
- improved capacity and inventory utilisation;
- avoidance of legacy-provider limitations or change costs;
- ability to licence the platform externally; and
- strategic control over the customer journey and roadmap.

These benefits may be substantial, but they require measured before/after data during the pilot.

## 8. Recommended decision path

### Continue, but change the investment hypothesis

Do not proceed on the assumption that 25,000 internal Tickets alone justify an independent Glacier company.

Continue Sprint 31 and pilot preparation as a bounded validation programme because:

- much of the product is already built;
- Ice Rinks Australia provides a real controlled test environment;
- incubation reduces near-term overhead; and
- a real pilot can quantify operational value and demonstrate whether external organisers will buy it.

Until the Product Comfort Gate passes, continuation means local product, security, test and deployment-foundation work using already-authorised resources. It does not authorise paid production infrastructure, entity establishment or professional engagements.

### Apply spending gates

Before significant external expenditure:

1. Confirm the prior provider's full invoices, fee basis, GST, refunds and ancillary costs.
2. Replace cloud allowances with dated low/expected/peak estimates.
3. Ask the accountant to validate which Ice Rinks Australia costs are genuinely incremental.
4. Confirm insurance extension cost.
5. Obtain scoped legal/security quotes rather than spending upper allowances automatically.
6. Set a pre-pilot cash-spend cap approved by the founder.
7. Define metrics for labour saving, conversion, add-on revenue, errors and support burden.
8. Interview potential external organisers before establishing a standalone entity.

### Commercial spin-out gate

Do not spin Glacier into an independent operating company solely because the software is ready. Require evidence such as:

- successful Ice Rinks Australia pilot;
- at least two external organisations expressing serious validated demand;
- credible contracted/near-contracted annual volume approaching 100,000 Tickets or equivalent recurring revenue;
- demonstrated gross margin after support and infrastructure;
- acceptable security/legal/insurance cost; and
- a funded 12-month operating plan.

## 9. Immediate model inputs required

- exact prior-season provider invoices and whether $1 includes GST;
- Bookings as well as Ticket count and average Tickets per Booking;
- online versus walk-up share;
- gross Ticket and Product sales;
- domestic/international card mix and average order value;
- refund, dispute and failed-payment rates;
- existing accounting, insurance, email, device and staff capacity reusable by Glacier;
- estimated hours currently spent on reporting, reconciliation, support and provider workarounds; and
- likely external organiser volumes and willingness to pay; and
- current probability, timing, city/Event count and expected range for the next national Winter Festival.

Once supplied, these inputs should become a scenario spreadsheet and a full three-year cash-flow model rather than remaining narrative estimates.
