# Glacier Entity and Asset Ownership Plan

## Status and purpose

Working decision plan created during Sprint 31 preparation on 28 August 2026. It is not legal, tax, accounting or trademark advice. Final structure and transfer instruments require appropriately qualified Australian advisers.

Glacier is intended to become a distinct commercial enterprise rather than an undocumented extension of Ice Rinks Australia. Immediate standalone establishment is now being compared with documented incubation inside Ice Rinks Australia followed by a defined future spin-out. This plan prevents valuable assets and production accounts from being acquired casually under the wrong entity while that pathway is being designed.

## Principles

- Glacier's long-term operating and intellectual-property ownership must be deliberate and documented.
- Ice Rinks Australia may be the documented incubating owner/operator, pilot customer, related party or strategic participant. Its role and any ownership must be explicit rather than inferred.
- A temporary holder must be eligible, identifiable and willing to transfer the asset under written evidence.
- Domains, source code, trademarks, cloud accounts, Stripe accounts, customer contracts and data-controller obligations are distinct assets/responsibilities.
- Production infrastructure must not depend on one person's personal email, phone, payment card or undocumented recovery method.
- No structural assumption in this document authorises a tax, trust, shareholder or related-party decision.

## Immediate holding question

The existing trustee/trust may be able to register a `.com.au` domain if the actual registrant is a qualifying commercial entity and the requested name satisfies auDA's allocation criteria. An ABN is an identifier rather than an asset-owning person: the registrar must record the exact eligible legal registrant, including trustee capacity where applicable, and the correct eligibility identifiers. An ABN must not be used merely because it is available.

An `.au` licence can later be transferred to another eligible person/entity through a formal change-of-registrant process. At transfer:

- both current and proposed registrants must be eligible;
- the new registrant enters a new licence agreement and may pay a fee;
- remaining full licence years may transfer, subject to auDA rules;
- access to the registrar account alone does not change ownership; and
- the new Glacier entity must independently satisfy the applicable name/eligibility connection.

A `.com` has no Australian ABN allocation test, but registrant identity, payment evidence and written beneficial-ownership/transfer terms still matter.

## Recommended interim approach

Do not acquire a domain through Ice Rinks Australia's operating ABN by default.

For an important available name that may be lost before the Glacier structure is completed:

1. Confirm with the accountant or commercial lawyer which existing legal person/entity is the intended temporary holder and whether it is acting beneficially or as trustee.
2. Confirm that holder satisfies the registrar/auDA rules for any `.com.au` name.
3. Register through a Glacier-specific registrar account using the temporary holder's exact legal details and a controlled Glacier administrative email.
4. Record the domain in an interim asset schedule with acquisition date, cost, registrant, beneficial-purpose statement, renewal date and intended transferee.
5. Retain invoice, registration agreement, auDA eligibility basis, MFA recovery codes and registrar recovery evidence.
6. Execute the formal change of registrant after the new entity is established and eligible; update the asset register and account recovery contacts.

If the candidate is replaceable or unlikely to be lost, waiting for the new entity is administratively cleaner.

## Decisions requiring professional advice

- Whether Glacier should use a proprietary operating company, a separate IP-holding entity, a trust, or a simpler initial structure.
- Which existing trustee/trust can lawfully and appropriately hold assets temporarily.
- Tax, GST, capital-gains and duty consequences of transferring domains, code, trademarks or other IP.
- Whether consideration is required for an asset transfer and how related-party value is documented.
- Share ownership, control, beneficiary and succession arrangements.
- Whether Ice Rinks Australia contributes existing know-how/IP or merely acts as pilot customer.
- Employment/contractor IP assignments for every contributor.
- Privacy roles and customer contracting entity for the pilot.

## Domain-name decision gate

Before purchase:

- shortlist brand-led names without committing Glacier to a narrow product category;
- check `.com`, `.com.au`, `.au`, business-name and Australian trademark conflicts;
- record registration and normal renewal prices;
- identify the exact legal registrant and eligibility basis;
- confirm registrar MFA, account recovery, lock/DNSSEC and change-of-registrant process;
- avoid bundled hosting/email purchases; and
- have the user complete the registrar account, identity and payment steps.

## Entity planning sequence

1. Prepare a concise business/ownership brief for the accountant and commercial lawyer.
2. Decide the intended operating, IP and ownership structure.
3. Check/reserve company and business names as advised.
4. Complete preliminary trademark search and obtain advice before relying on the Glacier name.
5. Establish entities, ABNs, banking and governance records.
6. Transfer or newly acquire assets into the intended holder.
7. Provision production accounts under entity-controlled identities and billing.
8. Execute contributor IP assignments and related-party agreements.
9. Confirm privacy, customer-contract and merchant entity before any real pilot data/payment.

## Exit gate

Production deployment may be technically prepared with placeholders, but no live customer, payment or production-data service should be activated until:

- the contracting/merchant and data-responsible entity is identified;
- domains and core accounts have a documented legal holder and recovery owners;
- source-code/IP ownership is evidenced;
- required transfers/assignments are completed or governed by written interim arrangements; and
- legal/accounting advice has resolved the flagged structure and related-party issues.
